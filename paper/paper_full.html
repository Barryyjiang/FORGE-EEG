The Electrode Budget:
A Wearable Centric EEG Benchmark

Barry Jiang · Mishti Broor · Sammy Mustafa · Fyzeen Ahmad · Chau Trần

Northwestern University, Evanston, IL, USA

Interactive explorer BibTeX Results data Code

Abstract

Wearable EEG replaces a dense laboratory cap with a handful of electrodes at fixed, convenient sites. Whether that trade is acceptable is usually argued from electrode count alone. We benchmark the other half of the question: when the number of electrodes is held fixed, how much decodable signal remains at a given site, and how does that cost depend on the paradigm? Across {{meta:nTasks}} tasks and {{meta:nSources}} public datasets — a 22-channel motor-imagery dataset at two and at four classes, an 8-channel P300 speller, a 12-class SSVEP speller, a simultaneous 115-scalp-plus-8-in-ear motor-execution recording, nine nights of scalp-and-ear sleep, a 48-person cognitive-workload headset, a simultaneous scalp / around-ear / in-ear auditory-attention recording, and a 123-person emotion-recognition recording — we re-run one fixed classical decoder per task on every montage, with exactly k electrodes on each side of a comparison. The channel budget looks cheap: reduced montages retain {{stat:mi_leftright_22ch:retainedPct}}% of the motor-imagery reference, {{stat:p300_8ch:retainedPct}}% of P300, {{stat:ssvep_8ch:retainedPct}}% of SSVEP, {{stat:motor_execution_ear_vs_scalp:retainedPct}}% of motor execution, {{stat:sleep_staging_local:retainedPct}}% of sleep staging, {{stat:stew_workload_14ch:retainedPct}}% of cognitive workload, {{stat:auditory_attention_earsaad:retainedPct}}% of auditory attention and {{stat:faced_emotion_123:retainedPct}}% of emotion recognition. Placement is the larger effect. In one session, with the same decoder and the same channel count, a sensorimotor scalp patch reaches {{pair:inear_vs_senso:meanA}} AUC while eight in-ear electrodes reach {{pair:inear_vs_senso:meanB}} (paired Δ {{pair:inear_vs_senso:delta}}, {{pair:inear_vs_senso:sign}}). Yet the ear is not useless: at a matched six channels the same wearable site loses only {{pair:sleep_scalp_vs_ear:delta_abs}} κ on sleep staging and gains {{pair:sleep_ear6_vs_ear14:delta_abs}} κ when all fourteen ear electrodes are used. The cost of a wearable is a property of the signal, not of the electrode: broad, slow sleep activity reaches the ear, a focal sensorimotor rhythm does not, and a speech envelope that even the full scalp only partly tracks is hardest of all for twelve in-ear electrodes.

1What this benchmark measures

An electrode montage is a spatial sampler. Two questions hide inside one comparison: how much information do k electrodes carry, and how much does it depend on which k? Only the second question is about wearables, and it can only be answered with the first held fixed.

The benchmark is a declarative cross-product of dataset, paradigm, protocol, montage and decoder. A task is one (dataset, paradigm, protocol) entry with one fixed classical decoder; a montage is a named set of electrode sites at a given channel count. Every comparison on this page puts exactly k electrodes on both sides, so a difference is a difference in placement.

  • Equal-N subsets. Region montages, max-spread (farthest-point) subsets and random subsets all have exactly k channels. Random subsets are the chance control for "does this montage beat an arbitrary one".
  • Paired differences. Deltas pair each participant with themselves (or each night with itself, for sleep), so between-subject variance cancels. Every interval quoted here is a paired t interval at 95%.
  • Measured wearables first, proxies labelled. The in-ear and ear montages of motor execution, sleep and auditory attention are electrodes that were physically recorded alongside the scalp electrodes, not nearest-neighbour stand-ins for a product. The workload headset has no ear recording, so its four- and two-electrode frontal sets are scalp proxies; they carry fidelity: proxy in every result file and in the explorer, and are never described as a device.
  • Full cohorts. 9, 8, 10, 6, 9, 48 and 15 subjects/nights: the complete public datasets used here, not a convenient subset.

2Results

2.1  The channel budget: returns flatten early

More electrodes help, and then they stop helping much. In motor imagery the max-spread subset climbs from {{cell:mi_leftright_22ch:distributed_k4:score}} at four electrodes to {{cell:mi_leftright_22ch:distributed_k8:score}} at eight and {{cell:mi_leftright_22ch:distributed_k16:score}} at sixteen, against a full-cap reference of {{stat:mi_leftright_22ch:reference}} at 22 electrodes. Eight electrodes placed over the motor cortex reach {{cell:mi_leftright_22ch:region-sensorimotor_k8:score}}, which is {{stat:mi_leftright_22ch:retainedPct}}% of the reference — a paired loss of {{stat:mi_leftright_22ch:delta_abs}} AUC, 95% CI {{stat:mi_leftright_22ch:ci}} ({{stat:mi_leftright_22ch:sign}}).

How much a montage can afford depends on how many things it is asked to distinguish. The same nine people and the same cap, cued for four classes instead of two, reach {{stat:mi_4class_22ch:reference}} accuracy at the full 22 electrodes and {{cell:mi_4class_22ch:region-sensorimotor_k8:score}} at the same eight sensorimotor ones — {{stat:mi_4class_22ch:retainedPct}}% of the reference, against {{stat:mi_leftright_22ch:retainedPct}}% for two classes. The paired loss grows from {{pair:mi_cap_vs_senso8:delta_abs}} to {{pair:mi4_senso_vs_full:delta_abs}} (95% CI {{pair:mi4_senso_vs_full:ci}}, {{pair:mi4_senso_vs_full:sign}}). Feet and tongue are represented near the midline, and the sensorimotor patch is lateralised over C3 and C4, so asking it for two more classes costs more than the electrode count alone would suggest. “Eight electrodes are enough” is a claim about a task, not about a head.

2.2  Placement is the larger effect

Wu 2020 is the cleanest placement experiment available: one session per participant, six participants, a 115-channel scalp cap and eight in-ear electrodes recorded at the same time. With eight channels on both sides and the same decoder, the sensorimotor patch scores {{cell:motor_execution_ear_vs_scalp:region-sensorimotor_k8:score}} while the frontal and temporal patches score {{cell:motor_execution_ear_vs_scalp:region-frontal_k8:score}} and {{cell:motor_execution_ear_vs_scalp:region-temporal_k8:score}}, and the ear scores {{cell:motor_execution_ear_vs_scalp:wu2020_inear_k8:score}}. The spread across placements is larger than the entire cost of dropping the other 107 electrodes: the full cap scores {{stat:motor_execution_ear_vs_scalp:reference}} against {{cell:motor_execution_ear_vs_scalp:region-sensorimotor_k8:score}} for the best eight ({{pair:cap_vs_senso:delta}} AUC, 95% CI {{pair:cap_vs_senso:ci}}), while moving those same eight electrodes from motor cortex to the ear costs {{pair:inear_vs_senso:delta_abs}} AUC.

2.3  The wearable gap is task-dependent

The same ear site behaves very differently on a fast, focal rhythm and on slow, broad sleep activity. On motor execution, eight in-ear electrodes lose {{pair:inear_vs_senso:delta_abs}} AUC against the matched eight scalp electrodes (95% CI {{pair:inear_vs_senso:ci}}, {{pair:inear_vs_senso:sign}}). On sleep staging, six ear electrodes lose {{pair:sleep_scalp_vs_ear:delta_abs}} κ against the matched six scalp channels (95% CI {{pair:sleep_scalp_vs_ear:ci}}, {{pair:sleep_scalp_vs_ear:sign}}), and adding the remaining eight ear electrodes recovers part of it: all fourteen reach {{pair:sleep_ear6_vs_ear14:meanB}} κ, a gain of {{pair:sleep_ear6_vs_ear14:delta}} (95% CI {{pair:sleep_ear6_vs_ear14:ci}}, {{pair:sleep_ear6_vs_ear14:sign}}).

2.4  Decoder choice rivals montage choice

The same eight P300 channels score {{pair:p300_decoder:meanA}} with a generic log-variance decoder and {{pair:p300_decoder:meanB}} with xDAWN plus Riemannian decoding: a gap of {{pair:p300_decoder:delta}} AUC (95% CI {{pair:p300_decoder:ci}}, {{pair:p300_decoder:sign}}), which is larger than any montage effect measured in that task. SSVEP, the one task that is naturally scored as information transfer rate, tells the same story: {{stat:ssvep_8ch:itrReference}} bits/min at the full eight-electrode cap against {{stat:ssvep_8ch:itrBest}} bits/min for the best four electrodes. Motor imagery shows the same pattern in miniature: at the same eight sensorimotor channels, Riemannian TSLR beats log-variance by {{pair:mi_decoder:delta_abs}} AUC (95% CI {{pair:mi_decoder:ci}}). A montage comparison is only interpretable with the paradigm's standard decoder in place, which is why one decoder per task is fixed in advance here and never tuned per montage.

2.5  Three paradigms that test the rule from a new angle

The last three tasks were added because each probes the placement rule from a direction the earlier ones could not, and each is scored under a protocol the others do not use.

Cognitive workload. STEW is 48 participants wearing a 14-channel Emotiv headset, with a 150-second rest recording followed by a 150-second SIMKAP multitasking recording. Here the decoder never sees the test person, which is the protocol a wearable product would actually face. Under it the full 14 channels reach {{cell:stew_workload_14ch:full_14_k14:score}} AUC and a four-electrode frontal “glasses-like” subset keeps {{stat:stew_workload_14ch:retainedPct}}% of that, a paired loss of {{pair:stew_glasses_vs_full:delta_abs}} AUC (95% CI {{pair:stew_glasses_vs_full:ci}}, {{pair:stew_glasses_vs_full:sign}}). The same four electrodes beat a four-electrode posterior subset by {{pair:stew_glasses_vs_posterior:delta}} AUC (95% CI {{pair:stew_glasses_vs_posterior:ci}}, {{pair:stew_glasses_vs_posterior:sign}}), and they sit at the 90th percentile of random four-electrode draws. Site, not count, is doing the work — and here the useful site is the front of the head, not the back.

The task also carries a confound that no analysis can remove. Rest was always recorded before the task, so the two conditions sit on opposite sides of a session boundary. A label-free control that asks only “first half or second half of this recording?” recovers a substantial share of the above-chance margin the rest-versus-task decoder uses (Table 6). The workload result is therefore reported as rest-versus-SIMKAP condition recognition, never as a validated mental-workload score. That the decoder transfers to an unseen person at all is itself evidence that much of what it reads is gross and universal rather than neural.

Auditory attention. Ear-SAAD is the only public dataset that records 29 scalp, 19 around-ear and 12 in-ear electrodes at the same time while a participant follows one of two talkers, so it tests the ear site directly instead of by proxy. At the paper’s 60-second decision window, twelve in-ear electrodes decode at {{cell:auditory_attention_earsaad:in_ear_12_k12:score}} against {{cell:auditory_attention_earsaad:scalp_12_k12:score}} for the twelve most spread scalp electrodes: a paired loss of {{pair:aad_inear_vs_scalp12:delta_abs}} (95% CI {{pair:aad_inear_vs_scalp12:ci}}, {{pair:aad_inear_vs_scalp12:sign}}). At nineteen electrodes the around-ear array loses {{pair:aad_around_vs_scalp19:delta_abs}} against a matched scalp nineteen (95% CI {{pair:aad_around_vs_scalp19:ci}}, {{pair:aad_around_vs_scalp19:sign}}). This is the largest wearable cost in the benchmark, and it is the one that matters most, because attention decoding is the application that motivates ear-EEG in the first place.

It is also a latency cost rather than a wall. Twelve in-ear electrodes climb from 0.519 at a one-second decision window to {{cell:auditory_attention_earsaad:in_ear_12_k12:score}} at sixty seconds and 0.844 at ten minutes, while the matched scalp twelve goes from 0.548 to {{cell:auditory_attention_earsaad:scalp_12_k12:score}} and 0.967. The ear does not fail to carry the signal; it needs longer to decide, which is exactly what a lower signal-to-noise ratio predicts.

The ear also runs out of electrodes long before the scalp does. Sweeping the channel budget at a fixed 60 s window, four in-ear electrodes already reach {{curve:aad:in_ear:4}} against a twelve-electrode ceiling of {{curve:aad:in_ear:12}} — eight more electrodes buy 0.009. The around-ear array is flat from two electrodes on ({{curve:aad:around_ear:2}} to {{curve:aad:around_ear:19}}). The scalp reference over the same budgets climbs from {{curve:aad:scalp:2}} to {{curve:aad:scalp:19}}. Adding ear electrodes is not a route to better decoding; changing the site is.

Emotion. FACED is the largest cohort in the benchmark: 123 participants, 30 scalp electrodes, and short videos chosen to elicit a particular emotion. Binary valence decodes at {{stat:faced_emotion_123:reference}} balanced accuracy against a 0.5 chance level — a real effect, significant across participants, but a small one. Four posterior electrodes reach {{cell:faced_emotion_123:posterior_4_k4:score}}, a paired loss of {{pair:faced_posterior_vs_full:delta_abs}} (95% CI {{pair:faced_posterior_vs_full:ci}}, {{pair:faced_posterior_vs_full:sign}}). The interval includes zero: at four electrodes this task costs nothing measurable.

The placement result points the opposite way from workload, and it is the useful part. A glasses-like frontal four scores {{cell:faced_emotion_123:glasses_4_k4:score}}, below the posterior four ({{pair:faced_glasses_vs_posterior:delta}}), and sits only at the 65th percentile of twenty random four-electrode draws while the posterior four sits at the 80th. Frontal electrodes are where a glasses frame would put them, and for this task they are the wrong choice: the answer to “where does the signal live” is a property of the paradigm, not of the head.

That cost has to be read against the margin. Retaining {{stat:faced_emotion_123:retainedPct}}% of a score that sits only seven points above chance is not the same claim as retaining {{stat:ssvep_8ch:retainedPct}}% of {{stat:ssvep_8ch:reference}}, and the benchmark's headroom column exists so the two cannot be confused. A task whose whole effect is small cannot support a strong statement about what a wearable preserves, only a weak one.

3What would make these numbers wrong?

A benchmark is only as useful as its failure modes. Two questions decide whether a montage result is trustworthy: can the decoder learn anything from the labels it was given, and were the two montages really evaluated on equal terms?

The first is answered with permutation controls. Each task's headline decoder and channel subset is re-run on shuffled labels — block-permuted for the continuous sleep task, since a circular shift there preserves the stage runs and the band-power drift and leaves the null near κ 0.13 — with 100 shuffles for motor imagery, SSVEP and motor execution, 20 for P300 (whose null standard deviation is small) and 200 block permutations for sleep, on every night. Every null lands within ±0.05 of its task's chance level (ROC-AUC 0.5, accuracy 1/12 for SSVEP, κ 0 for sleep), and the report refuses to write if a control leaves that band.

The second is answered by construction: every comparison on this page uses exactly k electrodes on both sides, references and reduced montages are scored on the same trials with the same folds, and the interval around each delta is computed from the paired per-participant differences rather than from unpaired group means. Each control covers the full cohort of its task, so the null is measured on the same subjects as the result it validates, and the sleep track's null permutes whole blocks of epochs — a circular shift there leaves the stage runs aligned with the drift of band power and is not a valid null.

4Discussion

What the results say

  1. Placement is the main effect, and it is task-dependent. Holding the channel count fixed, moving eight electrodes from motor cortex to the ear costs {{pair:inear_vs_senso:delta_abs}} AUC on motor execution but only {{pair:sleep_scalp_vs_ear:delta_abs}} κ on sleep staging. The difference is the signal: sleep is broad and slow and reaches the ear, while a focal sensorimotor rhythm does not.
  2. The channel budget itself is cheap above roughly four to eight electrodes. Every task keeps {{stat:sleep_staging_local:retainedPct}}–{{stat:ssvep_8ch:retainedPct}}% of its reference score with a reduced montage, and the curves flatten well before the cap is exhausted (Figure 2). Reporting "how many channels" without "which channels" therefore answers the less interesting half of the question. The one exception is auditory attention, which keeps {{stat:auditory_attention_earsaad:retainedPct}}% at matched channels: a lower signal-to-noise ratio is not free, it is only slow.
  3. Decoder choice can dominate montage choice. On identical P300 channels, swapping the decoder moves the score by {{pair:p300_decoder:delta}} AUC (Figure 5). A montage benchmark that ignores the paradigm's standard decoder measures decoder mismatch, not electrodes.

Limitations and scope

This is an electrode-subset benchmark, not a simulation of a product. It measures how much decodable signal is present at a site under a fixed classical pipeline. It does not model a particular wearable's amplifier, reference scheme, impedance or motion artefact; those are hardware questions, and the natural next step is a forward-model study validated against the simultaneous scalp-and-ear recordings used here.

  • Decoders are classical and fixed. No pretrained weights, no fine-tuning, no per-montage search. The point is to compare electrode layouts on equal terms, not to win a leaderboard — and the size of the decoder effects above suggests deep models would add a second axis of variation worth treating separately.
  • Spelling is not communication. SSVEP is reported in accuracy as well as information transfer rate, because a wearable BCI is judged by the latter; the ITR ranking of montages can differ from the accuracy ranking.
  • One non-EEG axis is missing. Comfort, gel-free operation and setup time are the reasons wearables exist; none of them appear in these numbers.
  • The workload task is confounded by design. STEW recorded rest before the task, so its two conditions sit on opposite sides of a session boundary and the released data cannot separate workload from time in session. Table 6 measures the drift and the text reports the task as condition recognition. A dataset that interleaved the conditions would settle it; none of the public workload sets here does.
  • Two cohorts are small. Motor execution has six participants and sleep nine nights. Their paired intervals are reported rather than hidden, and both are wide enough that the sign of the effect is the claim, not its size.
  • A c-VEP track was evaluated and dropped. The public datasets expose stimulus-bit events rather than command-level labels, so the generic pipeline is not a valid decoder for that paradigm. Reporting it would have measured the pipeline, not the montage.
