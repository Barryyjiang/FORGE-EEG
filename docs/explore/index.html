How many electrodes does a wearable actually need?

A lab EEG cap has 64–128 electrodes. A consumer wearable has two to eight. This benchmark fixes the number of electrodes and asks what is left: how much accuracy comes from how many electrodes, and how much from where they sit?

The idea in ninety seconds

An EEG cap is a spatial sampler. A wearable is a very small one.

EEG does not read thoughts. It records the summed electrical activity of millions of neurons, blurred by the skull and scalp. The decoder’s job is to find a pattern in that blur that reliably predicts what the person is doing.

1

The brain makes electricity

When neurons fire together, their voltages add up. At the scalp the result is millionths of a volt — tiny, but measurable with a good amplifier.

2

Electrodes sample a mixture

Each electrode sees a blurred combination of many sources. More electrodes give the decoder more views of the same mixture — but placement decides which views it gets.

3

A model learns the pattern

The decoder is trained on labelled trials and scored on held-out ones. The gap between the full cap and a wearable is the price of the channel budget.

Channel count

More channels, more information

Going from four to eight electrodes usually helps. But returns flatten: eight well-placed electrodes recover about 91% of the 22-channel motor-imagery score, and four occipital electrodes recover 98.5% of the SSVEP score.

Channel placement

Where they sit can matter more

In the same motor task, eight electrodes over motor cortex score 0.739. Move the same eight to frontal or temporal scalp and the score falls to 0.544 or 0.525; move them into the ear and it reaches 0.464.

03 · What it means

Three things the benchmark makes hard to ignore

The headline is not “wearables are bad” or “wearables are fine.” It is that the cost depends on the task, the decoder, and — at small channel counts — very strongly on placement.

0.74 vs 0.53 same eight channels

Placement is the main effect

Keep the channel count fixed at eight and move the electrodes: motor cortex scores 0.739, temporal scalp 0.525, and in-ear 0.464. Spreading electrodes to maximise coverage is not a substitute for being over the right source.

0.54 → 0.86 P300, two decoders

Decoder choice rivals montage

A generic log-variance decoder sits near chance on P300; xDAWN reaches 0.86. SSVEP has the same structure: covariance and band-power are not the right tool, and CCA is. The “best montage” depends on using the field’s standard decoder.

−0.275 AUC at matched 8 channels

Wearables are task-dependent

Ear-EEG, two tasks: motor execution collapses at matched eight channels, while sleep loses only 0.056 κ at matched six. Broad, slow waves reach the ear; focal sensorimotor rhythms do not.

Accuracy returns quickly — then flattens

Best non-random reduced montage, shown as a percentage of the full-cap score. Four to eight well-placed electrodes recover most of the accuracy in every task where a dense cap exists.

04 · Why you can trust it

What would make these numbers wrong?

A benchmark is only as useful as its failure modes. Every task gets a shuffled-label control, every site comparison uses exactly the same channel count on both sides, and every delta is paired by subject. The controls below all sit within the benchmark’s 0.05 tolerance of chance.

Shuffled labels should land at chance

Each decoder is re-run on permuted labels (whole 20-epoch blocks for sleep, where a circular shift is not a valid null — it preserves the stage runs and the band-power drift). If it can still learn, the real result is contaminated — or the control is broken. Every null below is within 0.05 of its task’s chance level.

What every comparison controls for

These are the design rules, not footnotes. Each one removes a way a montage result could be an artefact of the evaluation.

  • Matched channel counts. Region, spread, random and wearable comparisons use exactly k electrodes on both sides, so a difference is placement, not count.
  • Paired by subject. Deltas pair each participant with themselves; the ear and sleep comparisons report paired 95% confidence intervals.
  • Measured wearables only. The ear comparisons use electrodes that were physically recorded alongside the scalp cap, not nearest-neighbour proxies for a product.
  • One decoder per task, fixed in advance. The pipeline is the field standard for the paradigm and is never tuned per montage.
  • Full available samples. 9, 8, 10, 6 and 9 subjects/nights across the nine tasks — the complete public cohorts, not a convenient subset.

05 · Methods & reproduction

One data change, one decoder, one report

The benchmark is a declarative cross-product of dataset, paradigm, protocol, montage and decoder. A task is one (dataset, paradigm, protocol) entry; a decoder is one registry entry. The runner and leaderboard pick both up automatically.

How a track is evaluated

1 · dataPublic dataset
2 · montageEqual-N subsets
3 · decoderFold-wise fit
4 · reportΔ vs reference
  • Equal-N: every geometric condition has exactly k channels; random seeds are the chance control.
  • Nested spread: farthest-point order gives a prefix of length k for a monotone accuracy-vs-k curve.
  • Paired deltas: comparisons pair on subject (or subject-session where a dataset has sessions), so between-subject variance cancels.
  • Chance is per row: ROC-AUC 0.5, accuracy 1/n, Cohen’s κ 0.
  • Resumable: one CSV per track × protocol; re-running skips completed files.

Reproduce the page and the numbers

The site reads results/benchmark/*.csv and writes a single JSON bundle. No result is typed into the HTML by hand.

# benchmark, sleep, controls, quality, report
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\run_all.py

# rebuild the site data (numbers)
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\build_site.py

# optional: real EEG illustrations
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\build_site_signals.py

# preview locally
python -m http.server 8000 --directory docs

What this measures

  • Decodable signal at an electrode site. Each row fixes the pipeline and the channel count, then changes only where the electrodes sit.
  • Measured wearable electrodes. The in-ear and ear montages are physical recordings made alongside the scalp electrodes, not geometry proxies.
  • Classical decoders, one per task. No pretrained weights and no fine-tuning; the same estimator is refit from scratch inside every fold.
  • A public-data result. Every recording is an open dataset with a published protocol; the benchmark asks a narrower, montage-focused question.
